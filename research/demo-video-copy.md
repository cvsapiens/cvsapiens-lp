# What to say in a short product demo video

Research brief for the 40-second hero demo (2026-10-30). Sources are named per point; gaps are stated.

**Gaps.** No primary demo-script guidance was found from Copyhackers, April Dunford, Demand Curve, CXL or HubSpot. Dunford and HubSpot points come from podcasts and third-party summaries. Arcade's figures are self-reported and correlational.

## Structure

- **Make the point in the first 5 seconds; no brand intro.** NN/g video usability: intros of "5 seconds or less", "start strong". An Unbounce expert panel: the first 5 seconds decide whether people keep watching.
- **Key message in the first half.** Wistia: videos under a minute average 52% engagement. Vidyard 2022 (1.78M videos, via MarketingProfs): 66% finish videos under a minute. Arcade 2026 benchmarks: front-load value.
- **Say the pain, show the product.** Loom: problem, solution, next step. Demand Curve: hero visuals show "the product in action". Muted viewers only get the picture, so the product is on screen early.
- **One task done, not a feature tour.** Loom says so directly; Dunford (secondhand) calls the feature walkthrough the most common pitch failure. Arcade: lowest completion on demos heavy with promo language and jargon.
- **One call to action that matches the page button.** Loom; Demand Curve: the CTA is "the next step to fulfilling the claim in your header".

## Language

- "You", short sentences, active voice, no stock words ("unlock", "supercharge"). Harry Dry, Marketing Examples.
- Exact numbers beat vague claims (Arcade): use the product's real string, e.g. "3 of 6 required shown".
- **Play down "AI".** Washington State University, 2024 (Cicek et al., 1,000+ US adults): the words "artificial intelligence" in a product description lowered purchase intent through lower emotional trust, more so for high-risk purchases. Say what it does and the safeguard instead.
- **Moderate pace.** A 2007 study (Computer Speech & Language): 178 wpm lowered comprehension against 155 wpm for native and non-native listeners. Vendors plan 140–150 words a minute.

## Tone

- Warm and plain. NN/g tone-of-voice study: trust explained 52% of the variation in desirability; casual, conversational, moderately enthusiastic tones did best; humour is "extremely risky" in a serious category.
- No fake urgency (Harry Dry: "Don't exaggerate"; FTC 2022 dark-patterns report, via law-firm summaries). No promises of interviews or offers.

## Hero and autoplay

- Muted autoplay is always allowed in Chrome, sound is not (Chrome autoplay policy; Wistia docs for mobile and Safari). Design for sound off.
- Put the key words on screen and offer captions. Verizon Media/Publicis 2019 survey (5,600+ people): 69% watch with sound off in public; 80% say captions make them more likely to finish (self-reported).
- Controls, a pause button (WCAG 2.2.2 for motion over 5 seconds), a "watch with sound" control that restarts at 0:00, and the essentials repeated as page text (NN/g).
- A custom poster frame (Wistia: about 30% higher play rate, internal data), showing the result rather than a mid-transition frame.

## Script budget for 40 seconds

75–85 words, hard cap 90: about 130 wpm over ~36 s of speech, leaving the end card to hold for 2 s or more.

## Words

- **Use:** you/your, paste, job post, this job, tailored, the job's own words, the real match string, what's missing, checked against your resume, cover letter, interview prep, start free.
- **Avoid:** AI-powered, magic, revolutionary, effortless, seamless, game-changer, cutting-edge, unlock, unleash, supercharge, empower, leverage, optimize, dream job, guaranteed, beat/hack the ATS, stand out from the crowd, 10x, "in seconds" (unless measured), limited time, don't miss out; idioms that trip second-language listeners (nail it, ace it).

## Mistakes that cost conversion

Logo or brand intro, feature tour, information only in the voiceover, the best part saved for the end, buzzwords, a fast read, vague or several CTAs, outcome guarantees.
